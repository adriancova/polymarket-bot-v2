## summary

A public, unauthenticated RTDS client that normalizes the 30-second and
60-second Chainlink TWAP streams into the frozen `ReferenceTwapObserved`
contract, delivered as a new `packages/polymarket-public/src/rtds/**` subtree
reachable at the `@polymarket-bot/polymarket-public/rtds` subpath. WP-070's
market-adapter surface is untouched; the two adapters share this package's
injected ports, its `boundDetail` bound, its reconnect-backoff function and its
`Feed*` reason-code vocabulary, and nothing else.

What it does:

1. **Subscription wrapper.** Connects to `wss://ws-live-data.polymarket.com`,
   sends one `{"action":"subscribe","subscriptions":[…]}` frame covering the
   configured windows, sends the documented client heartbeat (the text frame
   `PING`, every 5 seconds), and reconnects with capped exponential backoff,
   resubscribing on every attempt.
2. **Normalized TWAP events.** `symbol` and `windowSeconds` are explicit on
   every event; the window is cross-checked against the topic that carried it
   and a disagreement is refused rather than resolved. The economic value is
   `full_accuracy_value` divided by `10^18` with exact decimal arithmetic — the
   floating `value` is never read anywhere in the subtree.
3. **Stream-gap and first-update behaviour.** RTDS publishes no snapshot,
   history or replay, so nothing is fabricated across a gap: a reconnect opens a
   typed `FeedGapDetected` whose adapter-side record says in two literal fields
   that it is **not recoverable from the venue**, and the first update of each
   series after a (re)connect carries `firstObservationOnSubscription` plus a
   MEASURED `unobservedInterval` — or, when that first update is not newer than
   the last one before the break, a typed `unobservedIntervalUnavailable` while
   the obligation stays outstanding until an observation can bound it
   (remediation R1-M2). No missed-update count is ever stated, because
   the publication cadence is undocumented and the venue page forbids inferring
   it. This adapter cannot emit `FeedResynchronized` at all — there is no
   authoritative TWAP snapshot to apply, and the event pins
   `authoritativeSnapshotApplied: true`.
4. **Staleness as data quality.** A `FeedStale` domain event (once per episode),
   a `quality` block on every observation, and a `metrics()` struct of plain
   typed values. No logger, metrics client, or observability package is imported.

200 new tests after remediation round 1 (was 172): 135 colocated unit tests and
65 offline contract tests driven by the frozen `WP-000` fixture plus two
provenance-labelled fixture files this package owns.

## assumptions

1. **`ReferenceTwapObserved.feedId` is the RTDS topic.** The field is documented
   as "Oracle or feed identifier the TWAP was published under". RTDS does not
   publish the Chainlink Data Streams feed ID (that is credentialed, and the
   page says reports "do not include symbol or window labels"), so the topic —
   `crypto_prices_twap_thirty` / `crypto_prices_twap_sixty` — is the identifier
   the update was actually published under on this transport. It is also a valid
   `CodeString` and it carries the window, so it cannot silently disagree with
   `windowSeconds`.
2. **`quality` rides alongside the payload, not inside it.**
   `ReferenceTwapObservedPayloadSchema` is frozen and strict; completeness is a
   fact about this feed's reception, not about the venue's observation.
3. **A negative exact value is a problem, not an event.** The page calls the
   field signed; the domain requires `NonNegativeDecimalString`. The refusal is
   typed and carries the raw value rather than clamping or dropping.
4. **An out-of-order observation is published, a duplicate is not.** Ordering is
   the gateway's business via `(gatewayEpoch, ingestSeq)`, so a late observation
   is real data flagged `outOfOrder`; republishing an instant already published
   would double-count one TWAP.
5. **Two receipt-side bounds are this client's, not the venue's**
   (`duplicateWindowPerSeries` 64, `maxTrackedSeries` 256). Both consequences
   are stated where enforced and pinned by tests.
6. **The CLOB SDK's reconnect constants are applied to RTDS as policy** (250 ms
   base, 30 s cap). Polymarket publishes no RTDS reconnect policy; reusing the
   function keeps one backoff shape in the repository.

## deviations

1. **`FeedResynchronized` is not emitted, ever, and no builder for it exists.**
   The event pins `authoritativeSnapshotApplied: true` and RTDS offers no
   snapshot to apply. Instead `acknowledgeUnobservedInterval({generation})` lets
   the caller clear the adapter's gap state, generation-checked, publishing
   NOTHING. This is the honest reading of ADR-009 §6 ("a TWAP-dependent strategy
   must halt on an RTDS gap rather than interpolate or backfill"). A reviewer
   should judge this explicitly.
2. **`reconnectWhenStale` defaults to `false`**, unlike the market feed's
   `true`. Driven by D-4/RTDS-U1, not by preference.
3. **Own problem/emission types rather than WP-070's.**
   `PublicMarketProblemCode` and `NormalizedPublicEventAny` are closed unions in
   files this package may not edit, and `feed/signals.ts` hardcodes
   `source: "polymarket"`, which would be wrong provenance for an `rtds` event.
   The reason-code VOCABULARIES (`FEED_DISCONNECT_REASONS`, `FEED_GAP_REASONS`)
   are imported and re-exported so a gateway aggregates on one vocabulary.
4. **Configuration and state failures reuse `PublicMarketConfigurationError` /
   `PublicMarketStateError`.** `PolymarketPublicErrorCode` is a closed union in
   `src/errors.ts`, which is WP-070's; adding an RTDS code would mean editing
   it. Reusing the two keeps one catchable `PolymarketPublicError` taxonomy.
5. **`payload` is optional on the envelope schema and required on the update
   schema.** Classification order: a payload-less frame is reported by its topic
   and type rather than as a shapeless envelope. Pinned by a test.
6. **An unusable PUBLISHER timestamp clears `venueTimestamp` and is counted**
   (`metrics().invalidPublisherTimestamps`) instead of costing the observation.
   The event is built from the payload's observation time; the raw frame is
   preserved regardless.

## known_risks

1. **RTDS-U5 is a derivation, not a venue fact.** If Chainlink's lookback
   window is not the interval immediately preceding the observation timestamp,
   `windowStartAt` is wrong by construction (`windowEndAt` would still be
   right). Nothing in the frozen artifacts or on the current page resolves it;
   the page explicitly says the sampling boundaries are unpublished.
2. **The gap is unrecoverable by design.** `requiresAuthoritativeSnapshot` is
   pinned `true` on the event, and for this feed the obligation can never be
   discharged from the venue. WP-120 must not wait for a snapshot that will
   never exist: it should read `feed.openGap.recoverableFromVenue === false`,
   halt TWAP-dependent strategies per ADR-009 §6, and then call
   `acknowledgeUnobservedInterval` to clear the state.
3. **A quiet feed and a dead feed are indistinguishable by default.** With
   `reconnectWhenStale: false`, a socket that silently stops delivering is
   reported (`FeedStale`, `metrics().stalenessMs`) but not replaced. That is the
   conservative choice under RTDS-U1, and it puts the decision on the operator.
4. **Series-tracker eviction resets first-observation history.** At more than
   `maxTrackedSeries` live series, the least recently updated one is evicted and
   counted; if it reappears, its next observation reports
   `firstObservationEver: true` — true of this adapter instance, not of the
   venue. Documented at the field and pinned by a test.
5. **Duplicate detection is windowed.** A redelivery older than
   `duplicateWindowPerSeries` observations is published as a late observation
   (flagged `outOfOrder`), not suppressed.
6. **`observationAgeMs` spans two clocks** (this host's and the venue's) and can
   legitimately be negative under local clock lag. It is a diagnostic, and is
   documented as one.
7. **Multi-symbol subscriptions receive every symbol.** Following the venue's
   own advice, `filters` is omitted when more than one symbol is wanted, so the
   adapter publishes symbols the caller did not name (never dropping them
   silently). WP-120 filters on `payload.symbol`.

#### R1-M1 — the Chainlink observation time is now read as Unix epoch milliseconds, and only that

**Probe reproduction (against the unmodified candidate `e14cdc7`).** The
reviewer's probe was run first, verbatim, and reproduced exactly:

```
normalizeRtdsInstant(1234)                    -> {"status":"ok","value":{"iso":"1970-01-01T00:20:34.000Z","epochMs":1234000}}
normalizeRtdsInstant("2026-08-28T12:00:00Z")  -> {"status":"ok","value":{"iso":"2026-08-28T12:00:00.000Z","epochMs":1787918400000}}
normalizeRtdsInstant("2026-08-28")            -> {"status":"ok","value":{"iso":"2026-08-28T00:00:00.000Z","epochMs":1787875200000}}
normalizeRtdsInstant(999999999999)            -> {"status":"ok","value":{"iso":"+033658-09-27T01:46:39.000Z","epochMs":999999999999000}}
```

The first and last are the SDK's numeric seconds heuristic (`value < 1e12 ?
value * 1000 : value`) applied to a field that is not documented in seconds; the
middle two are the generic date-string branch. The value decides `windowEndAt`,
`windowStartAt`, `outOfOrder`, and duplicate identity, so a mis-scaled one is
not an error a consumer can see — it is a confidently wrong window.

**Authority.** Both authorities agree and neither is ambiguous: the current page
prints `"timestamp": 1785178800000` in the direct-RTDS example (V6/D-2 above),
and the frozen report §10.3 writes the payload as `{symbol, value (number),
full_accuracy_value (string integer), timestamp (unix ms), window_s}` — **unix
ms**, in as many words. The SDK-normalized TWAP surface documented on the same
page (D-7) is a different surface and this adapter does not read it.

**Fix.** `src/rtds/values.ts` now carries two functions instead of one:

* `normalizeRtdsObservationInstant` — STRICT, for `payload.timestamp`. Accepts a
  JSON number that is a safe integer, or an all-digit string that parses to a
  safe integer; both are read as MILLISECONDS with no heuristic. Refuses
  everything else, plus negatives and anything outside the `Date` range. Absence
  stays `absent`. The caller turns any refusal into the existing typed
  `RTDS_INVALID_OBSERVATION_TIMESTAMP` problem carrying the raw envelope.
* `normalizeRtdsPublisherInstant` — the previous tolerant reading, unchanged,
  for the envelope's own `timestamp`. Nothing is computed from it, its
  documented type is `datetime | None`, and an unusable one still clears
  `venueTimestamp` and is counted (deviation 6 stands).

The ambiguous name `normalizeRtdsInstant` is gone, so no future caller can pick
the wrong one by accident. `venue.ts`, `normalize.ts` and `index.ts` were
updated to match, and both files' headers now state the asymmetry and why.

**Two decisions a reviewer should judge explicitly.**

1. **An all-digit STRING is accepted** as well as a JSON number, although the

#### R1-M2 — a generation break is an obligation, discharged exactly once

**Probe reproduction (against the unmodified candidate).** The reviewer's probe,
reproduced verbatim, with the later-newer observation added:

```
previous newest = T+60s (generation 1), first generation-2 observation = T:
  {"firstObservationOnSubscription":true,"sincePreviousObservationMs":-60000,
   "outOfOrder":true, unobservedInterval: ABSENT}
then a NEWER generation-2 observation at T+90s:
  {"firstObservationOnSubscription":false,"sincePreviousObservationMs":30000,
   unobservedInterval: ABSENT}
```

The break was consumed by an observation that could not measure it, and no later
observation could report it either, contradicting `result.ts`'s stated
semantics and acceptance criterion 2.

**The model, stated explicitly.** A new subscription generation creates one
per-series OBLIGATION to say something about the interval this feed did not
observe. It is discharged exactly once, and never silently:

| Situation | What the observation carries |
| --- | --- |
| First observation of the new generation, NEWER than the last pre-break one | MEASURED `unobservedInterval` (unchanged behaviour); obligation discharged |
| First observation of the new generation, NOT newer | `unobservedIntervalUnavailable` (`fromAt`, `reasonCode: "RTDS_NO_OBSERVATION_NEWER_THAN_GAP"`, `previousSubscriptionGeneration`); obligation stays OUTSTANDING |
| Any later observation while the obligation is outstanding, still not newer | `unobservedIntervalUnavailable` again — an outstanding break is visible on every observation, not only the first |
| The first later observation that IS newer than the pre-break bound, on that subscription or a further one | MEASURED `unobservedInterval` from the ORIGINAL pre-break bound; obligation discharged |
| No outstanding break | neither field |

The later-newer ordering the review asked to be pinned is therefore decided as
**it carries the measured interval**, with `firstObservationOnSubscription:
false` — that field keeps its exact meaning rather than being bent to fit. The
two fields are mutually exclusive, which a mechanical sweep over the ordering
space pins. `durationMs` is strictly positive by construction.

**Fix.** `src/rtds/observations.ts` gains an `OutstandingGap` per series and one
PURE `assessGap` function that both `judge` (which reports) and `remember`
(which records) call, so the reported quality and the retained state cannot
disagree. An already-outstanding gap takes precedence over a newer break,
because its `fromAt` is the older bound and measuring from it reports the whole
unobserved span rather than a suffix of it. `judge` still records nothing, so a
regressed observation refused at the domain boundary leaves the obligation
exactly as it was. `result.ts` gains `RtdsUnobservedIntervalUnavailable` and the
`unobservedIntervalUnavailable` field, both documented, and the
`unobservedInterval` doc comment now states the real rule instead of "present
exactly when the observation is first on a subscription". `feed.ts`'s "no
missing history is fabricated" section and `index.ts`'s exports were updated to
match.

### assumptions (remediation round 1)

Additional to the six above; none replaces them.

1. **An all-digit string is a legitimate spelling of unix ms on this field.**
   The page's direct-RTDS example shows a number; the report says "unix ms"
   without naming a JSON type. Accepting the digit string cannot mis-scale and
   cannot be confused with a date, and it is the form the SDK uses elsewhere.
   A reviewer who wants number-only strictness would change one branch.
2. **A pre-1970 observation time is not a Chainlink observation.** The only
   plausibility bound this round adds. Everything else about freshness stays
   operator policy under RTDS-U1.
3. **An outstanding break's ORIGINAL bound is the right `fromAt`.** When two
   breaks stack without an intervening newer observation, the reported interval
   spans from the last observation before the FIRST break, which is a superset
   of the unobserved stretch rather than a suffix of it.

### deviations (remediation round 1)

1. **The two RTDS instants are now read with different strictness**, by design
   and documented at both call sites. Round 1's DV6 tolerance survives only on
   the publisher timestamp.
2. **`RtdsObservationQuality` gained a field.** It is an adapter-side type this
   package owns, not a frozen contract; `ReferenceTwapObservedPayload` is
   untouched and quality still rides alongside the payload (assumption 2).
3. **The public surface lost `normalizeRtdsInstant`** and gained
   `normalizeRtdsObservationInstant` / `normalizeRtdsPublisherInstant`. Nothing
   outside this subtree imported it (WP-120 is not written yet), and leaving the
   ambiguous name in place is how the wrong parser gets picked later.

### known_risks (remediation round 1)

Additional to the seven above.

1. **A seconds-spelled observation timestamp now produces a 1970 window rather
   than a refusal.** It is honest and visible, but it IS published (flagged
   `outOfOrder`, failing any freshness threshold). If a future venue observation
   ever shows seconds on this field, the right answer is a venue-report entry
   and an explicit refusal, not a heuristic.
2. **`unobservedIntervalUnavailable` is a new field WP-120 must handle.** A
   consumer that branches only on `unobservedInterval` will see no interval on a
   regressed first observation, exactly as before. WP-120 must treat EITHER
   field as "TWAP coverage broke here" per ADR-009 §6.
3. **An outstanding break survives until an observation is newer**, so a series
   whose stream never resumes above the pre-break bound keeps reporting the
   unavailability. That is the intended reading — the gap really is still
   unmeasured — but it is unbounded in time, and it is cleared by series
   eviction (known risk 4 above) rather than by a timer.

2. **WP-120** must read `quality.unobservedIntervalUnavailable` as well as
