Round: **ADR-034**, docs only, authorized 2026-10-06 (`IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md`).
It answers three questions for
[ADR-034](../adr/ADR-034-order-lifecycle-semantics-pre-live.md):

- **(a) Order types** (§1): how FAK and FOK are expressed, and which SDK

## 0. Answers

| Question | Answer | Facts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (a) How FAK and FOK are expressed | **DOC:** `orderType` (`"FAK"` or `"FOK"`) is a top-level field of the `/order` body, outside the signed EIP-712 struct; market orders use `expiration` `"0"`; a market order never rests, and post-only applies to GTC and GTD only. **SDK 0.12.0:** `createLimitOrder` makes only GTC or GTD. FAK and FOK need `createMarketOrder`, whose BUY takes a USD `amount` (collateral) and whose SELL takes `shares` | F-79 … F-87 |
| (b) User-stream and REST timestamps | **DOC:** order and trade events carry a required `timestamp`, "Event timestamp in milliseconds"; order events also carry `created_at`, and trade events `match_time` and `last_update`, in Unix seconds. **REST:** an order read carries `created_at` (seconds) and the order's `expiration`, and no instant of its last status change; a trade read carries `match_time` and `last_update` (seconds), and an optional `match_time_nano` that SDK 0.12.0 drops. Placement and cancel answers carry no instant. **UNDOCUMENTED:** what instant `timestamp` names, whether it is monotonic per order, and how the push is ordered against REST answers | F-88 … F-98 |
| (c) Size precision | **DOC:** "Size decimals" is 2 for every tick size; the share quantity, or a market order's input amount, is rounded down to it; amounts are six-decimal integers. **SDK:** the 2-decimal share rounding (the packet's `roundDown(size, 2)`) is `quantize(toScaledAmount(size), roundConfig.size, Rounding.Down)`, with `size: 2` for all six ticks in `resolveRoundingConfig`. What the CLOB does with an off-grid amount is undocumented. **No fetched page gives Protocol V2 a different precision rule** | F-99 … F-104 |

## 1. Order types and the SDK methods that carry them

**F-79 (DOC). Limit-order lifetimes.** S-D01 lines 65-75:

  threshold. To set an effective lifetime of N seconds, use `now + 60 + N`. In

**F-84 (SDK). `createMarketOrder` carries FAK and FOK.**
- Signature (`dist/types-B9F4CyS-.d.ts` line 5798):
  `createMarketOrder(request: PrepareMarketOrderRequest): Promise<SignedOrder>`.

  ("Lowest acceptable price per share …"), and optional `orderType`, FAK or

- `src/actions/orders/market.ts`:

  - lines 133-136 and 386-391: a BUY with `maxPrice`, or a SELL with

  - **Without a limit**, lines 270-317 fetch the order book (`fetchOrderBook`,

- So **a FOK or FAK BUY amount is given in collateral (pUSD, as a USD
  notional), and a FOK or FAK SELL in shares.** This agrees with F-82.

**F-97 (UNDOCUMENTED). Monotonicity and ordering.** No fetched page states:
- what instant `timestamp` names beyond "Event timestamp";

**F-98 (INF). What the examples suggest.** Every documented example is
consistent with `timestamp` naming the instant of the change the event
reports:

emission time or delivery order.

## 3. Size precision

**F-99 (DOC). The precision table and its procedure.** S-D01:
- lines 258-265:

  | Tick size | Price decimals | Size decimals | Amount decimals |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `0.1` | 1 | 2 | 3 |
  | `0.01` | 2 | 2 | 4 |
  | `0.005` | 3 | 2 | 5 |
  | `0.0025` | 4 | 2 | 6 |
  | `0.001` | 3 | 2 | 5 |
  | `0.0001` | 4 | 2 | 6 |

- lines 282-287 (limit orders): "1. Express the price using no more than
  **Price decimals**. 2. Round the share quantity down to **Size decimals**.
  3. Calculate the USD amount. If it exceeds **Amount decimals**, round it up
  first to **Amount decimals + 4**, then down to **Amount decimals**."
- lines 291-292: "verify that the rounded share quantity still meets
  `min_order_size`, then convert both amounts to six-decimal integers."
- lines 1088-1091 (market orders): "Round the price and input amount down to
  the table's **Price decimals** and **Size decimals**. After calculating
  `takerAmount`, round it up first to **Amount decimals + 4**, then to
  **Amount decimals**. This preserves the maximum price for a BUY or the
  minimum price for a SELL."
- lines 1093-1101, the worked example: "A 10 USD BUY with a maximum price of
  `0.52` produces `19.2308` shares", with `"makerAmount": "10000000"` and
  `"takerAmount": "19230800"`.
- S-O02 lines 4779 and 4783: maker and taker amounts are "in fixed-math with 6
  decimals".

**F-100 (DOC, UNDOCUMENTED). What is rejected.** The documented rejections are
the price ("The CLOB rejects an order whose price does not conform to the
market's current tick size.", S-D01 lines 271-272) and the size ("Orders
smaller than the minimum order size are rejected.", S-D09 line 416). **No
fetched page says what the CLOB does with an amount beyond the table's
precision**: rejection, truncation or acceptance (U-53).

**F-101 (SDK). The SDK applies the table.**
- `src/actions/orders/context.ts` lines 14-31 (`resolveRoundingConfig`):
  `size: 2` for each of the six ticks; `amount` 3, 4, 5, 6, 5, 6.
- `src/actions/orders/amounts.ts` (digest `1938bfdc…`, equal to the
  `verified-2026-09-16.md` S-S1j body):
  - lines 34-71 (`computeLimitOrderAmounts`): `quantize(toScaledAmount(params.size),
    roundConfig.size, Rounding.Down)`, then the quote = size × price at
    `roundConfig.amount` decimals, rounded down;
  - lines 73-112 (`computeMarketOrderAmounts`): the **input amount** (a BUY's
    USD, a SELL's shares) is quantized down to `roundConfig.size` (lines
    84-88). The computed side is rounded **up** when a `maxPrice` or
    `minPrice` was given and down otherwise (line 89): `amount ÷ price` for a
    BUY (lines 91-100), `shares × price` for a SELL (lines 103-111).
- `src/actions/orders/fixed.ts`: `FIXED_SCALE = 1_000_000n` (line 4);
  `mulDiv` and `quantize` in `bigint` (lines 56-83). The inputs first pass
  through `PositiveDecimalNumberSchema`, which is a JavaScript `number`
  (bindings `shared.ts` lines 545-547). `toScaledAmount` then scales them with
  an `8 * Number.EPSILON` drift tolerance (`fixed.ts` lines 7, 17-25).
- `amounts.ts`, `limit.ts` and `post.ts` hash equal to the bodies
  `verified-2026-09-16.md` read at commit `983a10a7…` (S-S1j, S-S1o, S-S1m).
  The rounding has not changed since then.

So the SDK's 2-decimal share rounding is the documented "Size decimals"
column, and it is the same for every tick size. A limit order's size and a
market order's input amount are rounded down to 0.01.

**F-102 (INF, arithmetic on F-99). On-grid inputs need no rounding of the
quote.** A tick-grid price has at most "Price decimals" decimals, and a 0.01
share quantity has 2. In every row of the table, Price decimals + 2 equals
Amount decimals (1+2=3, 2+2=4, 3+2=5, 4+2=6, 3+2=5, 4+2=6). So a limit order's
quote (price × shares), and a market SELL's taker amount at an on-tick limit,
are exact once both inputs are on grid. A market BUY's share amount
(amount ÷ price) is generally not exact, and is rounded (F-101).

**F-103 (DOC). Protocol V2 and precision.**
- The V2 API migration page (S-D06, byte-identical to the 2026-10-05 S-D02
  behind F-63) adds the fill formula and the collateral target. It states no
  precision rule.
- The place-orders diff since 2026-10-04 (E-28) adds V2 identifiers and
  signing domains, and leaves the precision table and both rounding procedures
  unchanged.
- `verified-2026-10-05.md` F-64 observed the V2 canary's `mos` and `mts`
  equal to V1's.
- **No fetched page states a V2-specific precision rule.** Whether V2 crypto
  markets keep these parameters is still U-43 (2026-10-05).

**F-104 (DOC). The minimum order size's unit is still in conflict (C-7).**
S-D01 lines 250-251: "`min_order_size` is the minimum number of shares the
CLOB accepts for an order." S-D09 line 445 (byte-identical to 2026-10-05):

## 4. What this means for ADR-034 (pointers; the ADR decides)

1. **D1 (venue time).**
   - The OMS can receive the stream's `timestamp` (ms) for order and trade
     events (F-88, F-89), and `match_time` (s) for fills from REST reads (F-94,
     F-95).
   - It cannot receive a venue instant for a placement or cancel answer
     (F-96), or for the status change a REST order read reports (F-93).
   - A rule that needs one of those must say what happens when it is missing.
     The unit (C-25), what the instant names (F-97, F-98) and per-order
     monotonicity (U-52) are not documented, so the rule must fail closed if
     they are wrong.
2. **D2 (one executable quantity).** Shares and market-order input amounts are
   on a 0.01 grid for every tick (F-99, F-101). On-grid inputs make the limit
   quote exact (F-102). The CLOB's handling of off-grid amounts is
   undocumented (U-53).
3. **D3 (time-in-force).** GTC and GTD go through `createLimitOrder`. FAK and
   FOK need `createMarketOrder`, protected by `maxPrice` or `minPrice` so the
   SDK neither reads the book nor picks the price (F-84). `orderType` and
   `expiration` are unsigned wire fields (F-81, F-85).
4. **D4 (collateral target).** A FAK or FOK BUY is signed with makerAmount =
   floor₀.₀₁(USD amount) and takerAmount = amount ÷ maxPrice, rounded up at
   Amount decimals (F-82, F-84, F-101). What the venue then reports for such an
   order is U-51.
